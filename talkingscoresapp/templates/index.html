Where to find a MusicXML file

Many score websites let you download a score as a MusicXML file.

  • MuseScore.com has the most scores. Open a score, choose Download, then MusicXML. A free account is needed, and some scores need a paid plan.
  • Find a score on this site has over 2,000 free scores that open here directly: piano pieces, songs, string quartets and Bach chorales.
  • CPDL has choral music. Look for MusicXML in the list of files for each edition.

If you write music in a program such as MuseScore, Sibelius or Dorico, choose File, then Export, then MusicXML.

A free program such as Audiveris can turn a PDF into MusicXML. It can misread some notes, so the talking score may not match the printed page.

What a talking score includes

Each bar is written out in words: its notes, rests, rhythms, dynamics and repeats. You choose how it is worded. You can play any bars or the whole score, and download the talking score as a web page, plain text or braille.

It cannot open a PDF, a photo or a scan. Lyrics, chord symbols and figured bass are included only when the file has them.
