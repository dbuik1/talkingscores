Where to find a MusicXML file

Many scores can be downloaded as MusicXML from a score library.

  • MuseScore.com has the most scores. Open a score, choose Download, then MusicXML. A free account is needed, and some scores need a paid plan.
  • OpenScore has songs and string quartets that open here directly.
  • CPDL has choral music. Look for MusicXML in the list of files for each edition.

In most notation programs, choose File, then Export, then MusicXML.

A PDF can be turned into MusicXML by a scanning program such as Audiveris, which is free. Scanning programs misread some notes, so the talking score may not match the printed page.

What it does

It writes out the notes, rests, rhythms, dynamics and repeats a MusicXML file holds, bar by bar, in the words you choose. Any range of bars can be played, and the whole talking score can be downloaded as a page, as plain text or as uncontracted braille.

It does not read PDFs, images or scans, and it does not teach the music. Lyrics, chord symbols and figured bass are read only where the file records them.
