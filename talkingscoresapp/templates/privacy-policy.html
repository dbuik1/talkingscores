What is kept and for how long

A score file opened here stays on this server, alongside the reading written from it, so the link keeps working. Nothing is listed publicly and nothing is shared.

Score files

A file opened here is saved on this server under a long random name, together with the reading page, the plain text and braille versions, and the audio written for playback. That name is the only way to reach them: they are not listed anywhere on the site, and search engines are told not to index them.

They are kept until they are removed by hand. There is no automatic deletion running on this server, so a link stays good until someone clears out old scores.

The files may be opened by whoever maintains this server, to work out why a score did not read correctly. They are not passed to anyone else.

What the browser stores

Reading settings are kept in the browser rather than on the server, so they follow you from one score to the next on the same device and never leave it:

  • The colours, the text size, the playback settings and where you had reached in each score.
  • The choices made on the set-up page, so a second score starts where the last one left off.

Clearing the browser's site data removes them.

Cookies

Two cookies are set, both needed for the site to work:

  • csrftoken - proves a form was submitted from this site.
  • messages - carries a message, such as a rejected file, to the next page. It is deleted once shown.

There is no analytics code on this site and no third-party script of any kind, so nothing here counts visits or follows you between sites.

Email

An email address sent to talkingscores@gmail.com is used to reply and for nothing else. An enquiry may be discussed with other people or published as an example, with nothing identifying who sent it.

Security

Pages are served over HTTPS, the browser is told to load nothing from other sites, and uploads are checked for size and file type before anything reads them. That reduces the risk rather than removing it: files sent over the internet and stored on a server can be reached by someone who should not have them.

The code this site runs is open source and can be read on GitHub.
