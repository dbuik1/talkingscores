Privacy policy

A score file you open here stays on this server, along with its talking score, so the link keeps working. Nothing is listed publicly and nothing is shared.

Score files

A file you open here is saved on this server under a name generated from the file itself, together with the talking score page, the plain text and braille versions, and the audio for playback. That name is the only way to reach them: they are not listed anywhere on the site, and search engines are told not to index them.

Because the name comes from the file, anyone holding the same file arrives at the same talking score rather than making a second copy of it. Someone who already has your file can reach its talking score.

Files are kept until they are deleted by hand. There is no automatic deletion, so a link keeps working until someone clears out old scores.

Your file may be opened to work out why a score did not read correctly. It is not passed to anyone else.

What the browser stores

Your text size, colours, playback settings and place in each score are kept in the browser rather than on the server, so they follow you from one score to the next on this device.

The choices you make under Options are kept there too, so a second score starts where the last one left off.

Clearing the browser's site data removes them.

Cookies

Two cookies are set, both needed for the site to work:

  • csrftoken - proves a form was submitted from this site.
  • messages - carries a message, such as a rejected file, to the next page. It is deleted once shown.

There is no analytics code on this site and no third-party script of any kind, so nothing here counts visits or follows you between sites.

Email

An email address sent to talkingscores@gmail.com is used to reply and for nothing else. An enquiry may be discussed with other people or published as an example, with nothing identifying who sent it.

Contact

Security

Pages are served over HTTPS, each page tells your browser not to load anything from other sites, and uploads are checked for size and file type before anything reads them. That reduces the risk rather than removing it: files sent over the internet and stored on a server can be reached by someone who should not have them.

The code this site runs is open source and can be read on GitHub.
